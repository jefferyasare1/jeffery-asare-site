# About Street Photography

Much of my documentary and street work is made in the moment: in markets, on street corners, at festivals and everyday scenes of life in Accra and beyond. These are candid photographs, not posed portraits. I don't ask for permission before pressing the shutter, because doing so would change the very thing I'm trying to capture: a genuine, unguarded moment as it actually happened.

This is a long-standing and respected tradition in photography. Some of the most important documentary and fine art work of the last century, the kind that ends up in galleries and museums, was made exactly this way. It's a different practice from portrait or commercial work, where the person in front of the camera is a collaborator and a signed agreement makes sense. Street and documentary photography works differently, and is treated differently, because its value lies in honesty: showing life as it is, not as it was arranged to be.

That doesn't mean I photograph without care. I don't shoot people in situations that could embarrass or endanger them, and I don't photograph anyone as a means of mocking or exploiting them. When I choose to print and offer an image for sale, it's because I believe it holds real artistic or documentary value on its own, never because of who happens to be in it.

If you recognize yourself, or someone you know, in one of my photographs, I'd genuinely like to hear from you. Whether it's a question, a concern, or a request that a particular image not be sold, you can reach me directly at hello@jefferyasare.com. I take these conversations seriously and will always work with you in good faith.
