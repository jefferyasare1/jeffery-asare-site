Five paragraphs of bio before the page has said anything else: a long open with nothing to anchor on.

Location and Philosophy are paired side by side, three paragraphs each. That sets up a two-column rhythm.

…then Practice breaks it: same formula, but suddenly full-width and alone underneath. And its opening idea (unposed, no permission asked) already overlaps what Philosophy just said about shooting candidly.

The CTA and the shop strip are two more shapes again. Four sections, four different layouts, and not one photograph in between any of them.

Hero stays exactly as it is. Nothing added here, this is about what comes after.

Location, Philosophy, and Practice now share one shape: full width, same label and heading treatment, body text held to a readable column instead of stretching edge to edge. It's the shape Practice already uses, just applied to all three instead of one.

Reading down the page now feels like one statement instead of three layouts glued together, purely from proportion. No photography, no new visual device, nothing added.

Worth trimming Philosophy's opening line where it repeats what Practice says, that's a words fix, not a layout one.

Result One panel shape, used three times · zero new elements · same photography, same white space
This is a wireframe, not a finished design. The grey bars stand in for your actual sentences, scaled down so the shape of the page is easy to see at a glance. Nothing here is final; it's meant to make the conversation concrete rather than abstract.
