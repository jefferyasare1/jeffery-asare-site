Portfolio

I shoot on a phone. Not because it's all I have — because it's always there, and moments aren't. Some of these happened in a second — a turn of light, someone moving through a frame. Others I returned to, stayed with, waited on until they became what I needed them to be. All of them started with something that stopped me long enough to reach for my pocket.

Something stops me.
Every time.

My name is Jeffery Asare. I am a photographer based in Accra, Ghana, working professionally with a full kit, and personally with nothing more than an iPhone and a quiet obsession with the way light behaves when no one is paying attention to it.

I do not shoot with intention the way most photographers describe it: with mood boards and pre-visualised frames. I shoot because something stops me. A face. A shadow. The way a wall holds colour at the end of the day.

My work lives at the intersection of the ordinary and the overlooked. I am drawn to portraits that hold something back, to compositions that ask more than they answer, and to the kind of beauty that does not announce itself. I shoot on iPhone. I edit in Lightroom Mobile. I am not interested in what the camera cannot do, only in what I have not yet figured out how to see.

I want my photographs to slow people down, not to inspire in the broad, motivational sense, but to create a moment of pause. A second look. The feeling that something familiar has just revealed itself as strange, or something strange as deeply familiar.

My practice is built around the smartphone as a serious creative tool. I work with available light, ordinary scenes, and the kind of access that only comes from not carrying equipment. What I am after is not the extraordinary moment, it is the extraordinary quality hidden inside the everyday one.

Location

Accra holds
everything.

Ghana does not ask to be photographed. It simply exists, loud and textured and indifferent to the lens, generous light in the morning, sharp by noon, forgiving by evening. I do not travel to exotic locations looking for striking images. The ordinary streets here contain more than I have managed to photograph in years of trying, and that is enough for a lifetime.

Philosophy

The pause
is the work.

Most people walk past things. I am interested in the half-second before that, the moment something registers but has not yet been named. I use a phone because it closes the gap between noticing and capturing: no setup, no signal to the subject that something is being recorded, just attention. I edit the same way, intentional, entirely on the phone, finishing the sentence the moment started rather than manipulating it into something else.

Practice

The street doesn't
ask permission.

Much of my street and documentary work happens without asking first. A market stall, a corner, a face caught mid-thought: the moment exists because no one was told to hold still for it. I photograph with care, I don't chase people into situations that could embarrass or endanger them, and a photograph earns its place here because it holds something true on its own, never because of who happens to be in the frame.

If you recognise yourself in one of these photographs and want to talk about it, whether that's a question or a concern, write to me at hello@jefferyasare.com. I take that seriously.
